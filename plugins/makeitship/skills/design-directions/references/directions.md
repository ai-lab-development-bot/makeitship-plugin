# How directions are derived

A direction is a different answer to the problem, not a different skin. This file is how you
get several real answers — and avoid landing, three times over, on the category's default.
Work it in this order, in your own notes; the person sees only the result.

## 1. Name the rut

Before any candidate, write two lines:

- **The page this category always ships** for this request ("a developer section: dark, a code
  window, three feature cards, a terminal prompt").
- **Its predictable opposite** ("a light, airy editorial version of the same").

Neither is a direction. Add the AI clusters and your own prior from `anti-slop.md`: the first
palette that came to mind is spent. When the person's own words paint a picture (a product
name, a metaphor), its literal reading is part of the rut too: spend at most one candidate on
it.

## 2. Lock the identity (an existing product)

When the product already has a design language — in code, or on screen — write one sentence of
what is actually there, in real values: the ground and accent colours (hex), the loaded fonts,
the layout topology, the surface treatment (corners, borders, shadows, texture), the voice of
the copy. Skip an axis rather than invent it; never name an aesthetic family instead of values.

This **identity lock** binds every direction: they read as the same brand side by side. Inside
it, directions vary their expression (§4). A new token is allowed only as a flagged extension
(name, value, why — on the card), and a token keeps its role (`craft-floor.md`, Token roles).

A new identity — different palette, different type — is a **departure**, and happens only when
the person asked for one in so many words ("a completely new look", "redesign from scratch").
Unsure means no departure: three on-brand directions that feel close can be pushed; three
off-brand ones cannot be accepted.

## 3. Derive candidates from the audience's world

List five to seven candidates from the world of the people the surface serves: the objects,
places, rituals, publications, signage, data graphics and graphic traditions they know by
heart, and what their world looked like before screens. For each, one line on why it can carry
this request and one on how it looks (its ground, its type voice, its one recognisable mark).

- Span **at least three material families** (print, places, instruments, textiles, maps,
  signage…). More than three candidates from one family means you stopped at the most obvious
  artifact; keep digging.
- **Obvious candidates are allowed.** Obvious is not irrelevant; a strong obvious direction can
  be the recommendation.
- On **Operate and Read** surfaces, never a candidate taken from the tools the audience
  operates (a terminal, a cockpit, a control panel): on a working screen it comes back as a
  costume. On Persuade, at most one.

What a candidate becomes depends on §2:

- **New product, or a departure:** a candidate becomes a whole direction — palette, type,
  composition and its signature move.
- **Existing product (the lock binds):** a candidate supplies the direction's **signature
  move** and its expression inside the lock; on Operate and Read, only the move (`modes.md`).

Keep the two to five strongest, by how well they carry the product's truth and how the audience
would recognise themselves in them; recommend one.

## 4. Primary axes and the squint test

Directions differ on **different primary axes**, one each:

1. **Hierarchy** — which element commands the eye.
2. **Layout topology** — stacked, side by side, grid, asymmetric, overlay.
3. **Typographic system** — scale, case, weight logic (within the available faces under a lock).
4. **Colour strategy** — which palette role carries the surface: restrained, committed (one
   colour owns 30–60%), full palette, drenched.
5. **Density** — one thing per screen, balanced, dense.
6. **Structural decomposition** — merge, split, progressive disclosure, a different interaction
   model.

Then squint at them side by side:

- Under a lock, palette, type voice or rhetoric that drifted off the lock means a departure by
  accident: rework.
- Two directions on the same primary axis, or two one-line descriptions that rhyme, means one is
  not a direction: replace it.
- Three directions that are the same layout in three colours is one direction.

## 5. Regenerate without guidance

When the person asks for more ("show me others", "something else") and says nothing about what
to change, the whole set missed. Discard every direction shown — none returns as a variant —
and derive the next set from **other families** than the last one's, naming them. If two such
rounds miss, ask what quality is missing before a third.

When the person does say what to change ("bolder", "less corporate"), keep the set and move it
along that line.

## 6. Mixing

"Take B but with A's colours":

- **One direction stays dominant.** B keeps its sharp traits — its ground, its type, its
  signature move. A lends at most one or two named things.
- **A lends a discipline, not clothes.** What transfers is something like a palette's
  commitment or a grid's density, written on the result as its own line ("from A: the palette
  owns whole regions, not accents"). A lifted motif is not a mix.
- **Never average.** Dark and acid and serif references do not meet in the middle as cream,
  clay and a polite serif. If the two cannot live together, say so and show B with A's
  discipline next to A with B's.

Alongside the mix the person asked for, show one alternative mix they did not.
