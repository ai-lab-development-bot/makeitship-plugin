# Surface modes

The mode names what the visitor's success looks like on the surface being designed. It decides
how much the design may express and how much it must get out of the way. Choose it for every
request, from the surface — never from the product: a client's marketing site is Persuade, the
same client's admin is Operate, their help centre is Read.

| Mode | Surfaces | The visitor succeeds when they… |
|---|---|---|
| **Persuade** | landing pages, marketing sections, pricing, campaigns | decide and act |
| **Operate** | app screens, dashboards, admin, settings, editors, forms in a product | finish a task |
| **Read** | docs, articles, guides, help, changelogs | understand something |
| **Experience** | portfolios, galleries, showcases | are inside the work itself |

Say the mode in one line at the top of the round. A request that spans two (a pricing page that
opens a checkout) takes the mode of the screen in front of the person.

## Persuade

- **What the world may do.** Carry the page: its material can be the ground and the major
  surfaces, its type sets the headlines, its signature move can structure a section. Bold is
  welcome here.
- **What must survive.** The offer is clear in one line, the primary action is visible and
  works where it is (a search, a booking, an add-to-cart — not a link to it elsewhere), the
  page reads top to bottom as a page. A world painted over a page that no longer works is a
  poster, not a website.
- **Motion.** May carry the voice: one rehearsed focal moment rather than an entrance on every
  section (`motion.md`).
- **Copy.** Specific, in the product's words; proof over claims (`anti-slop.md`, Copy).

## Operate

- **What the world may do.** Lend four things and only these: type, palette, density and **one
  signature move** — service health drawn as a transit map, a schedule set in a timetable's
  column rhythm. It never supplies the layout, the navigation model or the controls.
- **What must survive.** Someone who uses tools like this one knows where everything is before
  they notice the world. Standard navigation (top bar, side nav, tabs, a command palette),
  standard controls, every state of every control, density where the task needs it. Familiarity
  is a feature; strangeness without a purpose is the failure.
- **Never a costume of the tools the audience operates**: a terminal's prompt, an instrument's
  bezel, ruled ledger paper as a table.
- **Colour.** Restrained by default — neutrals plus one accent for actions, selection and
  state — but colour still does jobs: a tinted shell, state colours, data series.
- **Motion.** 150–250 ms, conveying state, feedback or continuity; no page-load choreography.
- **Copy.** Orients, reports status, names actions. Headings are descriptive ("Revenue this
  month"), never aspirational ("Unlock your growth").

## Read

- **What the world may do.** Own the frame — the masthead, the navigation rail, the ground,
  section openers, how code, tables and notes are set — and leave the reading column calm.
- **What must survive.** Where the reader is and where to go next; a reading face at real
  contrast and a 65–75 character measure; standard forms for code, tables and figures.
- **Motion.** Almost none; feedback on controls only.
- **Copy.** Answers the reader's question first.

## Experience

- **What the world may do.** Lead from the first screen; the interface recedes.
- **What must survive.** A way in, a way out, and content that loads without a lock.
- **Motion.** May be the medium itself — still one idea, still with a reduced-motion path.
